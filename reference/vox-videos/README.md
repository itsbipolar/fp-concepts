## What that project was trying to build

A complete editorial studio for advanced Canadian financial-planning documentaries: business-owner and incorporated-professional decisions, estates, retirement, insurance structures, family wealth and cross-border questions. The primary audience is business-owner families; advisors form a second audience with a different editorial angle. Its pipeline connects topic selection, research, a claims ledger, plain-language scripting, narrated collage animation, review and a platform-specific delivery pack.

Its clearest creative preference is **objects performing the explanation**. The salary/dividend plan records rejection of a first version built around line items and graphs. Later versions move, cut and preserve cash through each stage, then reveal the less visible company tax. The user asked for quieter wobble, accurately proportioned cuts, plain words, technical backup outside the film, and a disclaimer ending. The v3 rendered contact sheet was inspected during this review; its visual style differs from Within reach.

## Selectivity and limits

The other repository bundles a separate private Summit snapshot. That snapshot, tax parameters, internal product roadmap and full household dossiers were not copied. Its high-level topic titles help generate questions; neither Summit nor study notes replace primary sources. Existing CFP notes in this project were not duplicated. Provider configuration, hooks, credentials, installed skills, binaries, media and dependencies were not imported.

Original claims scanners, automatic export gates, workbook generation, voice/transcription, analytics and posting integrations are **not installed by this transfer**. Their useful requirements are recorded in the active evidence/delivery workflow. No claim is made that they now run here. We retain this project's lean toolchain and adapt a tool when a concrete film needs it.

Market-size claims, retention benchmarks, platform limits and legal-policy summaries in the snapshot are historical research. Recheck them before relying on them; they are not a reason to force rapid edits, add legal text to every shot or pause an authorized draft build.
