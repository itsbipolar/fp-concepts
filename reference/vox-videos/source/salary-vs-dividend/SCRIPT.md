# Salary or dividend: where the tax hides (script)

Series: The Ledger · Pillar: 1 · The owner's money · Audience: families (owner-managers) · Cut: short (vertical, 47 s) · Summit: CORP-04

## Hook
Frame 0: the cash already landing on the company building (cut-outs). Spoken first line: "Say your company made an extra ten thousand dollars."

## Spoken
Say your company made an extra ten thousand dollars {c:ex-profit}. Pay it to yourself as a salary or a dividend? One of them gets taxed before you ever see it. Take it as a salary, and the company pays no tax on it {c:salary-deductible}. You pay the tax instead, and at Ontario's top rate you keep four thousand six hundred and forty-seven dollars {c:calc-salary-keep}. Take it as a dividend, and the company pays its tax first {c:calc-corp-tax}. You get a tax break for that, but you still pay tax on the rest, and you keep four thousand five hundred and thirty-nine dollars {c:calc-div-keep}. Almost the same, and that's on purpose: the tax system is built that way {c:integration}. So the real difference is what a salary gets you. It builds room in your RRSP {c:rrsp-earned}, and the C-P-P you pay on it builds a pension {c:cpp-salary}. A dividend gets you neither. It only feels cheaper, because the company paid part of the tax where you couldn't see it.

## Notes for the reviewer
- Plain words on purpose (ELI5). Every rule, rate and section number behind each line is in the Excel backup
  (`salary-vs-dividend-backup.xlsx`), with sources, the exact wording and live formulas.
- The video's example: $10,000 of extra active business profit, Ontario, an owner already in the top tax bracket,
  everything paid out the same year, a non-eligible dividend, the rules in force for 2027.
- The backup's "full picture" section adds what the video leaves out: CPP on salary (and the tax relief on it), EI,
  Ontario's Employer Health Tax and the Ontario Health Premium, with switches for the owner's situation.
- The $108 gap is specific to the top bracket in this example. At lower incomes the comparison changes, so the video
  doesn't generalise it. Quebec isn't covered.
