# Product marketing context (read by the social, copywriting, copy-editing and content-strategy skills)

The full playbook is `.claude/skills/fp-video/`. This page is the short version, so the generic marketing skills
always work inside it.

## What this is
An explainer-video channel about **advanced Canadian financial planning**: owner-manager and corporate tax, estates
and the two deaths, insurance used as a planning tool, retirement for the wealthy, trusts, families and
generations, cross-border (US) exposure, and Quebec. Its look is the paper-collage Vox style made by this kit,
narrated by a generated voice and written and reviewed by Mo.

The engine behind the topics and the verification is **Summit AI** (Mo's product, a private repo; snapshot in
`reference/summit/`). Summit's catalogue holds 390 planning analyses. Each one is a video idea, and Summit's
law-and-calculation write-ups are the research base.

## Whose voice
**Mo's personal brand** (LinkedIn, and YouTube under a version of his name). His firm approves every video and
post before it goes out. He can't give investment advice: investments appear only as they relate to planning
(`fp-video/references/COMPLIANCE.md` §1a).

## Audiences (in order)
1. **Business-owner families and incorporated professionals**: owner-managers with an Opco, a Holdco and a family
   trust; physicians' and dentists' professional corporations; partners in law and accounting firms. HNW
   (investable assets of about $1M+) to UHNW ($30M+).
2. **The people who advise them**: advisors at multi-family offices and wealth firms, CFP® professionals, CPAs, estate
   lawyers and insurance specialists. This is Summit AI's market, and LinkedIn is where it lives.
3. Sophisticated self-directed Canadians who have outgrown TFSA and RRSP basics.

## Positioning: the blue ocean
Canadian finance video is crowded at the retail end (budgeting, credit cards, ETFs, TFSA vs RRSP). The deep
planning topics (integration, RDTOH, the capital dividend account, estate freezes, the 21-year rule, post-mortem
pipelines, US estate tax for Canadians, IPPs, the insured annuity) appear mostly as law-firm blog posts and LinkedIn
text posts. **Nobody explains them visually with the actual statute on screen and the numbers worked through.**
That is the channel: *"the mechanics, with the receipts."*

## Voice
A calm, exact, slightly wry documentary narrator. It is never hype and never a guru. It shows its working: every
figure carries its source and its year. It says when the answer depends on facts, and when the law is unsettled.

## Hard limits (from `fp-video/references/COMPLIANCE.md`)
- **Education, never personal advice.** Nothing tailored to one viewer. Don't answer "what should I do" in comments
  or DMs.
- **No securities recommendations**, no tickers, no "buy" or "sell", and no promotional language regulators flag
  ("golden opportunity", "not to be missed", rocket emojis): CSA/CIRO Staff Notice 31-369.
- **No scarcity, urgency, fear or "secret" tactics.** No guaranteed outcomes.
- **CFP® marks** only as adjectives ("CFP® professional"), never in handles or names. Never imply FP Canada's
  endorsement, and never use "FP Canada" in the public brand.
- **Disclose AI**: the narration is a generated voice. Say so in the description and use each platform's AI label
  where it applies.
- Every number goes through the claims ledger (`fp-video/references/VERIFICATION.md`).

## Calls to action that are allowed
Follow or subscribe for the next mechanic; save it for your next meeting with your accountant; send it to your
advisor or co-owner; the full version is on YouTube; the sources are in the description. For the advisor audience
on LinkedIn: "this is the kind of file Summit AI reads". Only once Mo approves the wording, and only on LinkedIn.
