# Vision: FP Concepts

## Current vision: interactive illustrated consequences

Make complex Canadian financial decisions understandable through expressive illustrations that people can operate: **one choice reveals its connected consequences across the scene**.

The selected **Within reach** style uses bold colour, cut-paper shapes and recognisable, expressive subjects. A learner's action can reveal immediate cash effects, related taxes or costs, and future rights or benefits. Quantities give the visual story precision. The assistant researches the mechanism and invents the visual explanation; the owner should not need to supply the metaphors.

Success means a learner can change a financial choice and explain what changed now, what changed later and why. The active guidance is [Visual direction](VISUAL-DIRECTION.md) and [Interaction principles](INTERACTION-PRINCIPLES.md).

**Historical vision below:** the original machine-based proposal is retained as background. Its invented-machine, physical-metaphor and one-machine/one-idea constraints do not govern new work. The audience and goals of interactive education and shareable visual material remain relevant.

## The idea in one line
Explain Canadian financial-planning concepts the way Colorfigures explains a toaster: as a colourful, hand-inked
machine you can turn around, look inside and operate, except the machines are invented and **their mechanics are the
rules**.

## Why it works
- Financial rules are already mechanical: thresholds, rates, limits, tests, schedules, rollovers. They are just
  invisible. A weir, a tap or a marble gate makes them visible without dumbing them down.
- People remember what they operated. Pressing "Withdraw from TFSA" and watching the OAS stack stay whole teaches more
  than a paragraph on net income.
- It stands out. Wealth-management marketing is stock photos of couples on beaches and line charts. A hand-drawn
  machine with a working see-through is distinctive and shareable.
- One source of truth serves two jobs: the same figure is a **client-education tool** (in a meeting, on a phone) and
  a **marketing asset** (stills, turntable clips, carousel frames).

## Who it is for
- **Primary:** high-net-worth Canadian clients and prospects (business owners, incorporated professionals, executives,
  pre-retirees and retirees with $1M+ investable, families planning intergenerational transfers).
- **Secondary:** the advisor's own team, centres of influence (accountants, lawyers), and the broader audience on
  LinkedIn / Instagram / a newsletter.
- Tone: smart, warm, precise. Never cute at the expense of accuracy. Never salesy inside the figure.

## What a finished figure is
1. A self-contained HTML page (works offline, on a phone, embeddable).
2. A machine that is correct closed, in see-through, and from every angle (the Colorfigures standard).
3. Numbers that match a hand calculation from the fact ledger, with an as-of date and a disclaimer.
4. A brief (`figures/<slug>/BRIEF.md`) recording the rule, the sources, the mapping and the compliance pass.
5. Exports: three social stills and a turntable clip.

## Principles
1. **Truth first.** The machine can simplify; it can never contradict the rule.
2. **One machine, one idea.**
3. **Every number has a source and a date** (`docs/FACT-LEDGER-2026.md`).
4. **The misconception breaks on screen.** Each figure is built around the wrong belief a client actually holds.
5. **Education, not advice.** No recommendations inside figures; compliance pass before anything is published.
6. **Versioned sources.** v0 = the CFP crash notes; each new document or link becomes v1, v2... and feeds the catalog.

## What success looks like
- v0: the toolchain, the skill and the playbook exist and are proven by one shipped figure.
- A series of 6–10 figures covering the HNW questions asked most often (OAS clawback, RRSP vs TFSA, estate freeze,
  death and taxes, corporate insurance and the CDA, prescribed-rate loans).
- A simple gallery page and a monthly cadence of one new machine plus its social cut-downs.
