# Evidence and delivery for a financial film

Adapted from the user's [other video studio](../reference/vox-videos/README.md). This adds traceability to [our existing production workflow](VIDEO-WORKFLOW.md). It does not turn historical research into verified law or make a style calibration depend on a financial claims ledger.

## Keep the proof connected to the picture

For a financial film, keep a compact `claims.json` beside its brief and script, starting from [the local template](templates/VIDEO-CLAIMS.json). Give each factual statement or calculation a stable ID. A script marker such as `{c:salary-deductible}` links a sentence or shot to that record. Keep claim markers in source notes; remove them from spoken or visible copy. The marker convention is currently manual; this transfer installs no automatic script scanner.

For each material claim record:

- The exact statement, year, jurisdiction, status, and the visible/spoken wording that depends on it.
- The primary-source URL, publisher, retrieval date, relevant effective date and verbatim supporting passage. Study notes and Summit write-ups can point to evidence; they cannot substitute for it.
- Whether the basis is legislation, regulation, a court decision, an administrative position, practice or unresolved interpretation. This classification supports research judgment; it is not a mechanical rule that every court case forbids calculation or every statute guarantees a result.
- Whether the outcome can be computed under stated assumptions, requires a caveat, depends on facts or needs specialist review. Represent that uncertainty in the explanation.
- Which scene/beat uses it and which assumptions, source claims and calculation file support a derived number. Distinguish illustrative inputs from legal parameters.

An imported “verified” label is not verification in this project. Reopen and check the primary source for the film's stated scope. An inaccessible page stays unresolved. Different dates can be valid when clearly labelled; do not mix a future tax-year scenario with earlier contribution values without explicitly checking applicability.

## Reproduce the comparison

Keep the calculation model independent of animation, with input units and rounding rules. Include material employer and personal costs under a shared starting basis. If an excluded cost could change the answer, narrow the film's conclusion or show its effect; a footnote cannot repair a misleading visual takeaway.

For a quantified worked example, prepare the other repo's useful reviewer deliverable: **one workbook sheet** containing assumptions, every displayed/spoken value, source URLs and exact quotes, live dependent formulas, and comparison checks. Also keep an independently reproducible calculation or boundary checks; copying a stated result into a cell is not a live model. Workbook generation is not installed in this transfer. Build it when a film needs quantified review, and record the actual checks performed.

## Review and authorization

Separate research status, creative review and publication status. A successful render verifies a file; positive style feedback verifies a direction; neither signs a financial model. The user has responded positively to the current motion study. Copied approvals from the source repository apply only to its artifacts and are not reused here.

The source project records a personal-brand channel, general education rather than individual recommendations, and firm approval before public posting. Carry that publication context into a film's [delivery record](templates/VIDEO-DELIVERY.json). Keep identity/credentials, actual reviewers and approvals blank until supplied or recorded for that artifact. The repository name is not a public channel name or a claim of affiliation with FP Canada. Do not invent branding, credentials or endorsements.

Prepare scripts, models, drafts, renders and review packs within the user's authorized scope. Firm review concerns public posting; it does not require stopping this authorized internal work. No messages, submissions, uploads or posts are sent merely by preparing the pack. Applicable current rules and the user's actual firm requirements should be checked at publication time, rather than treating a copied policy summary as current legal advice.

## A useful delivery pack

Alongside the editable composition and render, save the final script, captions/transcript where applicable, a deduplicated sources list, illustrative assumptions, model/backup, thumbnail or keyframe, and a delivery record. Capture AI narration and any actual affiliations accurately. Do not label a silent film as narrated or claim a human review that did not occur.

An explainer and its derivatives should share the underlying verified claims, with an explicit subset and parent version for each cut. Shorter cuts must preserve caveats that matter to their conclusion. Recompose for vertical delivery rather than cropping the landscape scene. Recheck current platform specifications; the upstream social algorithms, length limits and benchmark targets are not adopted as facts.

## Learn and correct

Retain released versions and record a film's claim IDs, source dates, publication date and locations. Review claims when indexation, a budget, an enacted measure, a relevant administrative update or court decision affects them. Log an actual error, its correct sourced statement, affected versions and the correction action needed. Publishing those corrections still requires the applicable authorization.

If performance data becomes available, compare the film's actual retention and comprehension evidence against this channel's own prior work. The source repo's idea scores were editorial guesses, not audience data. Do not import them as measured demand or claim that a mandatory edit frequency improves learning.
