 Statements, ratios and the cash-flow fix high 
 Net worth statement Assets − debts as of a date . Snapshot.
 Cash flow statement Income − expenses over a period . Built from the budget. Surplus = savings, deficit = debt.
 Liquidity = Liquid assets Monthly expenses Months of expenses covered. Higher is better. Emergency fund target: 3–6 months (money market, cashable GICs, even a line of credit). 
 Debt service = Monthly consumer-debt payments Monthly after-tax income Excludes the mortgage. Target ≤ 20% . 
 Savings = Cash surplus After-tax income Target 5–10%+. Higher is better. 
 Client has a cash-flow problem: answer in this order
 1. Cut discretionary Travel, entertainment. Refinance high-interest debt (credit cards) to a cheaper facility. 
 2. Reorganise investments Interest-bearing assets inside TFSA/RRSP; dividends and capital gains in non-registered. 
 3. Increase income Last resort, but sometimes the "best available" answer. 
 Trap Debt consolidation lowers the monthly payment by stretching amortization, but total interest over the life of the loan rises sharply.

 FHSA flow
 Open 18+, resident, first-time buyer. Room only starts accruing once opened. 
 Contribute $8,000/yr; unused carry-forward capped at $8,000 (so max $16,000 in one year). No 60-day rule. Spouse can gift funds; no attribution. 
 Qualifying withdrawal Still first-time at withdrawal, written agreement to buy before Oct 1 of next year, occupy within 1 yr. Can combine with HBP on the same home. 
 Not used? Close by Dec 31 of year 15 or age 71 Transfer tax-free to RRSP/RRIF (uses no RRSP room) or withdraw taxable. 
 Trap Over-contribution tax 1%/month (like TFSA). Non-qualifying withdrawals don't restore room. No creditor protection. Interest on money borrowed to contribute is not deductible.
 FHSA ↔ RRSP FHSA → RRSP any time: tax-free, uses no RRSP room. RRSP → FHSA: tax-free but uses FHSA room, and the RRSP room is gone for good.
 Bankruptcy (BIA) RRSPs are protected (except contributions in the last 12 months). TFSAs and FHSAs are not .
 LLP timing check First LLP withdrawal in 2019 → can keep withdrawing until January of the 4th year after (last withdrawal before Jan 1, 2023). Repayment of $5,000 over 10 yrs = $500/yr; repaying $300 means $200 added to income. Typical use: fund your own education or exam costs now, repay when income rises.

 Selling depreciable property: CCA outcomes high 
 CCA is optional (any amount from nil to max). Half-year rule: only 50% of net additions qualify in the year of purchase. UCC = cost − CCA claimed.
 Compare proceeds with capital cost and UCC (last asset in class)
 Proceeds > capital cost Capital gain + full recapture
 Gain = proceeds − cost. Recapture = cost − UCC (100% income).
 UCC < proceeds ≤ cost Recapture only
 Recapture = proceeds − UCC. Report as income.
 Proceeds < UCC Terminal loss
 UCC − proceeds. Deduct from business income.
 Rule Subtract from UCC the lesser of proceeds and original cost. If UCC goes negative → recapture. Class empty with UCC left over → terminal loss.
 Wendy: cost $50,000, UCC $30,000 
 Sells for $20,000 → terminal loss $10,000. Sells for $40,000 → recapture $10,000. Sells for $60,000 → capital gain $10,000 + recapture $20,000.
 Rental property
 Only the building is depreciable, never the land: $950,000 with 75% building → $712,500 goes in the class.
 CCA can't create or increase a rental loss (net all rentals first).
 Rental sold above original cost All CCA claimed comes back as recapture , 100% income (e.g. $68,250). Only the excess over original cost is a capital gain (e.g. $550,000, 50% taxable).
