# Why can a dividend feel cheaper than a salary?

Provisional full-film script; approximately 60 seconds after natural pacing. Audience: incorporated Ontario owner. Internal research-pending draft, not final narration. Year: 2026. Exact passage narration is separately identified below.

## Opening and argument

The first image starts after an important event: a dividend approaches the owner's hand with only the personal-tax consequence visible. Interrupt it before contact; expose the company's earlier tax stage; return to the same handoff. The question is about an incomplete view, not a claim that dividends cannot leave more cash in a particular scenario.

## Full-film draft

Salary or dividend? Looking only at your personal tax leaves part of the story out. Start with the same cost to your company. {c:no-winner}

A reasonable salary for your work can reduce the company's taxable income. {c:salary-deductible} Your side includes income tax and, under this scenario, CPP contributions. {c:salary-cpp}

With a dividend, the company pays tax on its profit before that profit funds your payment. {c:dividend-after-corporate-tax} You then pay personal tax, with a dividend tax credit. {c:dividend-credit} That does not make every outcome equal.

Now look beyond today's cash. Salary can add to your CPP earnings record {c:salary-cpp} and create RRSP room for next year. {c:salary-rrsp-next-year} A dividend does not create those new additions. {c:dividends-no-new-earnings} Your earlier record and existing room remain yours. {c:existing-rights}

Start at the company. Follow it beyond today. {c:no-winner}

## Reviewer notes

- CPP and RRSP wording assumes applicable salary, earnings headroom, limits and adjustments. Existing rights persist in either choice. New RRSP room is not a deposit and the CPP record is not a cash balance.
