# The part before the handoff

Creative proposal for an internal qualitative draft; factual wording remains subject to the claims review. Audience: an incorporated Canadian owner wondering why a dividend can feel cheaper. Planned film: approximately 60 seconds, 1280 × 720, 30 fps.

## Visual argument

The opening deliberately starts the story too late: a dividend is already crossing into the owner's open hand. Interrupt that familiar image, go back to the company's profit, expose the company-tax stage, then replay the **same handoff**. Revisiting the same shot changes its meaning. A second replay compares salary from the same company-cost basis; a later-life match cut reveals the different records and opportunities the payment can create. No winner is declared. Choose this over proportional note-cutting or a two-sided tax ticket: those risk unsupported amounts or label-led storytelling.

## Representative passage: 14 seconds / 420 frames

Render as a self-contained reconstruction of beats 1–2. Test: can a viewer name the missing company-tax stage with sound off?

- **0.0–2.5 s / f0–74:** Owner fills right half; payment travels approximately 70 px toward the open palm, then stops 20 px short. Keep face and hand together. Text beside the hand: “Your dividend tax.” Company is outside this shot.
- **2.5–3.3 s / f75–98:** A 6-frame backward movement retracts the payment along its exact arrival path. Hard cut on that reversal to the company aperture. Large, brief “Before the dividend” establishes the change in time. This is editorial reconstruction, not a repayment.
- **3.3–6.8 s / f99–203:** Company dominates the left/centre. One whole, constant-size payment emerges from its aperture and travels diagonally down-left toward an offscreen tax destination; “Company tax on profit” follows close enough to identify that event. Let it exit completely. Baked notes remain untouched. The motion is an outflow, never a slice removed from the dividend token.
- **6.8–8.3 s / f204–248:** Hold on the company after that event. Change the temporal cue to “Then, the dividend.” Separate tax and distribution; nothing returns from the tax destination.
- **8.3–10.7 s / f249–320:** Match cut to the exact opening handoff position. Replay the same last 70 px and finish contact. “Same dividend, fuller picture” briefly makes replay explicit. 
- **10.7–14.0 s / f321–419:** Widen once to include company and owner. Settle with “Company tax” anchored left and “Your dividend tax” right. Main line: “Both belong in the comparison.” Hold at least 2 seconds. 

Recommended available voice: reuse the pinned source narration **16.56–19.78** (“Take it as a dividend, and the company pays its tax first”) at passage **3.3–6.52 s**, then source **19.78–23.02** (“You get a tax break for that, but you still pay tax on the rest”) at **8.3–11.54 s**. The latter refers to dividend tax-credit treatment; do not imply a particular saving. These excerpts require the film's factual review. “First” represents the economic sequence, not a statutory remittance timetable. Omit the source's categorical “It only feels cheaper” kicker. Sound: dry paper withdrawal, company outflow, soft handoff contact. Preserve breathing room and caption the actual excerpts.
